# Step 27 — Where each hand sits

Shades, on the on-screen keyboard, the five keys each hand is placed over, and marks each of
them with the finger that rests on it, worked out from the fingering the score already carries.
Adds `src/keyboard/handPosition.ts` and its test; touches `src/keyboard/PianoKeyboard.tsx`,
`src/keyboard/PianoKeyboard.css`, `src/keyboard/PianoKeyboard.test.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`, an e2e
spec, `DECISIONS.md` and item 9 of `MANUAL-CHECKS.md`. No dependency, and no change to the
score or its parser. Independent of step 22. One branch, `step-27-hand-position`, off `main`.

## Goal

The player's ask: "a feature that shows on the keys which fingers should be used … and how the
hand should be placed." Today the keyboard lights the key to press in the hand's colour, and the
finger lives only in the queue above it, as a coloured circle with no key attached. A child
reading the keyboard learns where to press but not with what, and never sees where the rest of
the hand should be waiting.

## Confirmed decision — the hand's position, not a drawing of a hand

Offered three options — a finger number on the key to press, the five-key position of each hand,
or a hand outline drawn over the keys — the owner chose **the position**. It carries the first
option inside it: the key to press is one of the five, with its number.

Rejected: **a drawn hand** (an SVG silhouette placed over the keys). It covers the keys it is
meant to point at, has to be refitted to each of the three keyboard widths, and says nothing a
shaded row with numbers does not. It can be revisited if the shaded row proves hard to read.
**The number on the key to press only** is the smaller step, and was folded in rather than built
first, because it is the same code with one key instead of five.

## How a position is worked out

`cicha-noc.musicxml` puts a `<fingering>` on every note, and the parser already reads it into
`Note.finger` (`src/score/parseScore.ts:166`). A finger on a key fixes the other four, on the
assumption that the hand sits over five neighbouring **white** keys:

- **Right hand:** finger 1 is lowest. Finger _k_ sits _k − f_ white keys from a note played by
  finger _f_.
- **Left hand:** finger 5 is lowest. Finger _k_ sits _f − k_ white keys from it.

Which note a hand's position comes from: **that hand's next fingered note at or after
`nextEventIndex`**. That is where the hand should be ready, so an idle hand — the left through
bars 1-2, the right through 3-4 — already shows where it will come in, and a move shows as the
shaded row jumping the moment the last note of the old position is played. Worked through the
piece this gives:

| Hand  | Bars              | Position | Anchored on |
| ----- | ----------------- | -------- | ----------- |
| Right | 1-2, 11-12, 15-16 | E4-B4    | thumb on E4 |
| Right | 5-6, 17-18        | B4-F5    | thumb on B4 |
| Right | 9-10, 13-14, 19   | A4-E5    | thumb on A4 |
| Left  | 3-4, 8            | E3-B3    | 5 on E3     |
| Left  | 7                 | F3-C4    | thumb on C4 |
| Left  | 19-22             | C3-G3    | 5 on C3     |

Bar 7 to bar 8 is a real move and back: the
thumb reaches up to C4 and the hand returns for G3 on 3. The rule shows it as two jumps, which is
what the hand does.

Rejected: **one position per phrase**, chosen so that it covers every note in the phrase. It
shows fewer jumps, but needs a notion of phrase the score does not carry, and it would place the
hand where no single note says to. **Fingering generated by the app** for notes that have none:
this piece has none missing, and it is a research problem of its own.

## What is drawn

For each hand with a position, its five white keys get a **light tint of the hand's colour**
(amber for right, teal for left, lighter than the existing expected-key colours, and clear of the
held sky blue), and each of the five shows its finger number, bold, above the note name that
step 13 put at the bottom of every white key. The key the engine is waiting for keeps its strong
hand colour and its number; a held key keeps its held colour and its number. The black keys
between them are not tinted: nothing in the position rests on them. Two `data-` attributes carry
the truth for the tests, as the existing ones do: `data-position-hand` and `data-finger`.

`PianoKeyboard` gains one prop, the positions to draw as `{ pitch, hand, finger }[]`, computed in
`App.tsx` beside `expectedNotes` and passed empty in exactly the cases `expectedNotes` is: while
the demo runs (step 17's comment at `App.tsx:477` gives the reason), and past the end of the piece.
The derivation is a pure function in `src/keyboard/handPosition.ts` taking the filtered score and
the index. It reads the **filtered** score, so "Left hand" practice shows only the left hand's
position, for free.

## Traps

- **Black keys.** The white-key rule is undefined when the anchoring note is itself a black key.
  This piece has none, but `Finger` is on every `Note` type, and step 22 brings other pieces.
  Recommended: anchor on the nearest white key below for the right hand and above for the left,
  so the other four still land on white keys, and let the black key carry its own number. Open
  question 5 asks whether that is worth doing now.
- **A same-hand chord** fixes the position twice, possibly inconsistently. This piece has none;
  take the chord's lowest note for the right hand and highest for the left (the hand's outer
  finger), and write that down rather than leaving it to the order notes happen to come in.
- **A position that runs off the keyboard.** The four-octave default starts at C2, and the lowest
  position here is C3-G3, so it does not occur; a key outside the range is simply not drawn.
- **Loop wrap.** "Next note at or after the index" is linear, as `upNext` is in
  `practiceView.ts` (DECISIONS.md, "deliberately linear"): on the last event of a loop the idle
  hand may show a position from beyond the loop. Accepted, for the same reason.
- **Note names.** Step 13 owns the committed keyboard screenshot. The finger number has to share
  the white key's bottom strip with the name without either being cut off at the narrowest
  preset (88 keys), and the screenshot is re-taken once.

## In scope

The derivation and its tests, the tint and numbers on the keyboard, the wiring in `App.tsx`, and
the committed screenshot.

## Out of scope

- **Fingering for notes that have none.** A note with no `finger` contributes nothing, and the
  hand takes its position from its next fingered note. Every note in this piece is fingered.
- **Changing the queue.** Its finger circles stay as step 4 left them. With the numbers on the
  keys it is partly redundant, and folding it (step 18) is already the player's control for that.
- **Thumb-under and finger crossings.** The rule re-anchors on every note, so a scale with a
  thumb-under shows as a jump per crossing. Correct, if busy. None occurs in this piece.

## What this makes harder later

No persisted state and no exported format, so nothing here is one-way. Two futures were played
forward. **A second piece (step 22)** with black keys or chords: the two traps above are where it
lands, and the rule for each is written down here rather than invented then. **Fingering the
player edits themselves:** the derivation reads `Note.finger` and nothing else, so an edited
fingering flows through unchanged.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Numbers on all five keys, or only on the key to press?** All five (recommended) is what
   "how the hand should be placed" asks for. If wrong, the keyboard is busier than a child wants,
   and the fix is one condition.
2. **Warn before a move?** (a) No warning: the tint jumping to the new position when the last note
   of the old one is played is the cue (recommended; cheapest, nothing else on screen). (b) A line
   under the keyboard, "Next: right thumb to B4". (c) The next position drawn as an outline while
   the current one is still in use. If (a) is wrong, a move catches the child out, and (b) is a
   small follow-up step.
3. **The idle hand.** Show where it will come in (recommended — it is where the hand should be
   waiting), or hide it until its turn? If wrong, the resting hand's tint is distracting, and the
   fix is one filter.
4. **During Listen.** Keep the keyboard showing only what sounds (recommended, as step 17 decided),
   or show the positions following the demo, so a child can watch the hand move before playing?
   If wrong, the second is a small follow-up that reuses this step's function with the demo's
   position.
5. **Black keys now or later?** Handle a black-key anchor now (recommended against: this piece
   has none, and code for a case with no test piece is untested), or leave it to step 22 with the
   rule above written down? If wrong, the second piece shows a misplaced hand until it is fixed.

## Decisions to record in `DECISIONS.md`

A new entry: a hand's position is five white keys fixed by one fingered note; it comes from the
hand's next fingered note, so an idle hand shows where it will come in; the key to press keeps
the strong colour and the rest of the position a light one; and the queue keeps its finger
colours, so the number on a key is a plain numeral. Amend "The keyboard is coloured by hand, the
queue by finger" to say the keyboard now carries finger numbers too, uncoloured.

## Gate

**Layer 1**, `src/keyboard/handPosition.test.ts`, against the parsed `cicha-noc` score: at the
first event, right hand E4-B4 on 1-5 and left hand E3-B3 on 5-1; at bar 5's first event the right
hand is B4-F5; at bar 7 the left is F3-C4 and at bar 8 E3-B3 again; past the last event, nothing.
**The bar-5 case is the one that fails if positions are taken from the first note instead of the
next one**, and the left-hand cases fail if the left hand is numbered like the right.

**Layer 2**, `PianoKeyboard.test.tsx`: given a position, those five keys carry
`data-position-hand` and `data-finger`, the black keys between them carry neither, and a held key
in the position keeps its `data-finger`.

**Layer 3**, an e2e spec over the virtual keyboard: at start the right hand's five keys show
1-5, and after playing through bar 4 the right hand's shaded keys have moved to B4-F5. Nothing in
the suite knows whether the numbers are readable; that is manual.
