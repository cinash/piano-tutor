# Step 28 — All of Beyer Op. 101, from the scan

Reads the public-domain Peters edition of Beyer's _Vorschule im Klavierspiel_, Op. 101, with
optical music recognition, has it proofread against the scan, and fingers every note — the book's
own printed digits where it prints them, the five-finger rule for the rest — so that every numbered
piece exists as MusicXML the app can parse. Adds Beyer pieces to `beyer_op101_musicxml/`, a
manifest of the book beside them, an import pass alongside the fingering tool of the
`beyer-lilypond-fingering` branch, and a Vitest test over the library; touches `DECISIONS.md` and
item 11 of `MANUAL-CHECKS.md`. No npm dependency; **Audiveris 5.11.0** is used as a tool, outside the
repository and the dev image. Depends on `beyer-lilypond-fingering` having landed on `main`. Worked
in **batches, one branch each** (below), starting with `step-28-beyer-omr-calibration` off `main`.

## Goal

The owner's ask, after the LilyPond route was found to cover only Nos. 8–31: "don't forget the plan
for the music recognition and generation from the scans", with fingering that "moves the hand not
too much — as it was in the original book", and the standing requirement of this collection that
a file carries `<technical><fingering>` for the hands that play it or is not kept. Outside
MuseScore there is no digital edition of the rest of the book; the only complete sources are scans.

## Depends on: the LilyPond branch

`beyer-lilypond-fingering` is being built in parallel and provides what this step extends rather
than re-creates: a Python five-finger-position fingering pass over MusicXML, regression-tested at
100% against the human-fingered PDMX pieces (Nos. 8–10 right hand, No. 38 both hands); `python-ly`
pinned; a non-destructive check that reports which hands of a file are fingered; and Nos. 8–31 as
MusicXML. The owner chose Python for these tools. **Do not start until that branch is on `main`**,
and read what it settled — where the tools live, how its files write repeats and clefs — because
this step follows its conventions rather than inventing a second set. Where this brief and that
branch disagree, raise it; do not silently pick one.

## The source: one scan, identified exactly

**Edition Peters Nr. 2721, edited by Adolf Ruthardt, Leipzig [1895], plate 8033** — IMSLP #81208,
from the MIT archive project, 600 dpi, cleaned and deskewed. The copy used while planning is the
archive.org mirror `imslp-im-klavierspiel-op101-beyer-ferdinand/PMLP31149-F_Beyer-Op101.pdf`,
10 301 776 bytes, SHA-256 `369d3800bc8ad42c97fb4312b92060060a6d42aee4e642c35de797fb65ff23c2`,
88 pages whose PDF page numbers equal the printed ones. It is not committed (10 MB); the manifest
records the hash so every page reference points at one file. Its last two pages are publisher's
advertisements naming twentieth-century composers, so this is a later reprint from the 1895 plates;
the provenance names the plates, which is what the notes come from.

Rejected, each for a reason a later reader might otherwise re-open:

- **Schirmer 1919, ed. Scharfenberg (IMSLP #164414).** Public domain everywhere, but scanned at
  200 dpi monochrome — a third of the resolution OMR gets from Peters, on the thin stems and small
  fingering digits it most needs.
- **Curci 1947 and Ricordi 1918 (Piccioli, Pozzoli; #464235, #610339, #863877).** IMSLP marks all
  three _Non-PD EU_: their editors died in 1961 and 1957. The owner writes in Polish; committing an
  editor's fingering that is still in copyright in the EU is not a risk to take quietly.
- **Carisch & Jänichen (#569346) and the unattributed #12916.** No identified editor, so no
  provenance to record.
- **Collecting MuseScore uploads instead of recognising a scan.** Most need an account, many a Pro
  subscription, most uploads are "all rights reserved" by the uploader even where the music is not
  (only the CC0 subset reached PDMX — four pieces), and their fingering is whoever uploaded it. Pieces
  the owner downloads anyway still enter through the LilyPond branch's check; this step does not
  wait for them.

Every file records its provenance in `<rights>`, as `cicha-noc.musicxml` does: first published
1850 (Mainz: Schott, per IMSLP), public domain; notes recognised with Audiveris 5.11.0
from the Peters edition above (IMSLP #81208, page numbers); proofread by whom and when; which
fingering digits are the edition's and that the rest were generated by the project's five-finger
rule. `<encoding><software>` names Audiveris 5.11.0 and the import pass.

## What a piece is, in this book

Read from the scan, not assumed:

- **Nos. 1 and 2 are for three hands**: a Theme and its variations (twelve for No. 1), the pupil's part
  for **the right hand alone** (No. 1, pp. 10–13) and **the left hand alone** (No. 2, pp. 14–17), on
  the page facing the teacher's (_Lehrer_ / _Schüler_). Variations are numbered _Var. 1_, _Var. 2_ …
  and restart per section.
- **Four-hand duets** — Nos. 3, 8, 10, 32, 41, 44, 63, 86 among those seen — print the teacher's
  _Seconda_ on the left page and the pupil's _Prima_ on the right. **The Prima is itself a two-hand
  part**, both staves in treble clef in the early ones. The LilyPond and PDMX Nos. 8 and 10 are
  Primas.
- **Solo pieces for both hands** from No. 12 (_Exercises for Both Hands_, p. 24), moving through
  bass clef, scales with the thumb passed under (_Exercise on Turning Over and Under_, p. 48),
  double notes (pp. 49–50), 8va passages, the keys of D, A, E and F major and A minor, and a
  chromatic scale (pp. 74–75).
- **The Peters numbering runs to 109** (No. 109, p. 76), where IMSLP describes the work as "106
  studies in 2 stages". Nos. 8–10 and 38 agree with the LilyPond and PDMX numbers (No. 8 is note for
  note the LilyPond No. 8). File names use **the Peters numbers**, and the manifest says so.
- **Outside the numbering**: _Exercises in Touch for the Right Hand_ (pp. 8–9), an _Appendix_ of
  finger exercises (pp. 77–83) and _24 Major and Minor Scales_ (pp. 84–86).
- **The book prints hand positions.** Many pieces, the early ones especially, open with a small
  _Handlagen / Positions of the hands_ diagram — the notes under each hand with their fingers — and prints fingering digits over
  some notes, not all. **Digits printed under the first bar of the early pieces ("1 2 3 4") are beat
  counts**, not fingers.

What is in scope of that list is open question 4.

## How the notes are recognised

**Audiveris 5.11.0** (released July 2026; installers for Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 as `.deb`, macOS,
Windows, and Flathub; each bundles its own Java runtime). Its batch mode is
`audiveris -batch -transcribe -export -output <dir> -sheets <pages> <book.pdf>`, and
`-constant org.audiveris.omr.sheet.BookManager.useCompression=false` exports plain XML instead of
`.mxl` (handbook: _Command line interface_, _Outputs / .mxl_). A book export writes **one file per
movement**, and a movement starts at an **indented system** — which is how Peters starts each
numbered piece and each variation, so a range of pages comes out already split. Its OCR languages
are not bundled; they are needed for titles and tempo words, not for notes, and the piece number
comes from the manifest, not from OCR.

**Where it runs** is open question 5. Batch recognition needs no display and could run inside the
dev container from the `.deb` unpacked into a scratch directory — untested; the image is Debian, the
package targets Ubuntu. Correcting a misread symbol is done in Audiveris's own editor, which draws the
recognised symbols over the scan and needs a display — the owner's host. CLAUDE.md's rule against
giving the container the piano does not bear on this either way.

**Fingering is not read by the OMR engine.** Audiveris has fingering detection behind a processing
switch that is off by default and documented as a guitar feature (_Fingering and Plucking_: digits
for the left hand, letters p-i-m-a for the right); its symbol set does include digits 0–5. Its
maintainer, asked about recognising fingering on a learning sheet (discussion #724, April 2024),
pointed at the switch and had no way to improve the recognition. And the beat counts under the early pieces would be read as
fingers. So the switch stays off, and printed digits are captured by a person, if at all
(question 3).

## Two files per piece: what the book says, and what the app plays

Each piece is kept twice. **The source** is the proofread recognition — the edition as printed:
repeats as repeat signs, the teacher's part where there is one, and only the digits the book prints.
**The derived file** is generated from it by the import pass: what the app loads. Keeping the source
is what makes the rest revisable — a better fingering rule, repeats taught to the app, a teacher's
part wanted for accompaniment — without recognising or proofreading anything twice, and it keeps
the book's digits apart from the generated ones for good. Rejected: **committing only what the app
plays**. Smaller, but every change above becomes re-recognition, and a generated finger could no
longer be told from a printed one. A human correction to fingering goes into the source as a digit,
never into a derived file, which is overwritten.

The derived file differs from the source in these ways, each already a precedent here:

- **Repeats are written out**, as step 21 wrote out Cicha Noc's (`DECISIONS.md`, "repeats are
  unimplemented"): the parser reads measures in document order, and the staff, the engine, the
  measure readout and the loop picker would all run past a repeat sign. With first and second
  endings that would mean playing both endings back to back — wrong music, not merely unrepeated.
  Rejected: implementing repeats in the app. It is the real answer the day a piece's bar numbers
  must match the child's book, and it is its own step.
- **Only the pupil's part**, as the first and only `<part>` — `parseScore` reads the first
  `<part>` and ignores the rest (`src/score/parseScore.ts:35`).
- **A left-hand-alone piece puts its notes on staff 2 of a two-staff part** with staff 1 resting.
  `parseScore` calls staff 2 the left hand and everything else the right (`parseScore.ts:112`), so
  No. 2 recognised as one staff would be practised as a right-hand piece with left-hand fingering.
  Rejected: teaching the parser to read the clef or a part name — an app change for one piece.
- **Every note of every hand that plays is fingered.** The LilyPond branch's check requires both
  hands, which a one-hand piece can never pass; it is extended to "the hands the manifest says play".

## Fingering: the book's digits first, the rule for the rest

The five-finger rule from the LilyPond branch — five adjacent scale steps per hand, the hand moved as
rarely and as little as possible — matched every fingered note of the right hands of Nos. 8–10 and of
both hands of No. 38. **It does not match the book's left hand in Nos. 8 and 9**: there the left hand
plays a single repeated G, which the rule fingers 5 (bottom finger on the lowest note) and Peters
fingers **2** in No. 8 and **3** in No. 9 — and No. 8's position diagram shows the same 2. With one
pitch in play nothing in the notes says where the hand sits; the book says. The PDMX files, whose
left hands carry no fingering, could not show this.

So the pass is **anchored**: a digit the book prints is kept, and fixes that hand's position at that
note — the same idea as step 27's _a finger on a key fixes the other four_ — and the rule fills only
the notes between anchors, choosing positions that agree with them. A note no five-finger position
between its anchors can reach — a stretch past a fifth, a thumb passed under that the book does not
mark — is left unfingered and listed, not guessed. The later pieces are where this bites: the
scales and chromatic exercises print dense fingering precisely because no fixed position covers
them, and No. 18's LilyPond transcription already had ten notes the unanchored rule could not
reach. What happens to a piece
with listed notes is question 3.

Rejected: **`pianoplayer`** as the filler. Measured on the same four pieces, it matched 69–88% of the
right hands and 20% of No. 38's left: it minimises motion over a short look-ahead and so shifts the
hand by a key where the book keeps it still. **The unanchored rule alone** is rejected by Nos. 8 and
9 above.

## Proofreading

OMR misreads — a dot, an accidental, a tie, a note a step off — and a child practising a wrong note
learns it. Every piece is compared with the scan by someone before it counts as proofread, and each
source file says in `<rights>` whether it has been and by whom. What automation contributes is
**where to look**, not a verdict: a measure whose durations do not add up to its time signature, a
note the fingering rule cannot place (often a misread pitch), a key or time signature that changes
where the manifest says it does not.

How much of the reading the owner does, and when a piece counts as done, is question 2 — and it is
the one the calibration batch exists to answer with a number rather than a guess.

## Batches

Recognising, proofreading and fingering 109 pieces is not one reviewable diff. Each batch is its own
branch off `main`, passes `npm run ci`, and goes through the code review on its own:

1. **`step-28-beyer-omr-calibration`** — the import pass, the manifest (number → pages → kind:
   right hand alone, left hand alone, both hands, Prima; the printed hand-position diagram; the scan's
   hash), the anchoring extension, the Vitest test, and **Nos. 8–31 recognised but not committed as
   pieces**: compared note by note with the LilyPond branch's transcriptions of the same numbers
   (pitch class and rhythm; the two disagree on octave and clef by design, see below). The result is
   the measured OMR error rate on 24 real pieces, reported to the owner. This batch is the same
   whatever questions 2–5 answer.
2. **Then the rest, in batches of about fifteen pieces in book order** — Nos. 32–46, 47–61, and so on
   to 109 — then Nos. 1–7 (the three-hand variations and the first duet, the most conventions at
   once), then whatever question 4 adds. **These wait for questions 2 and 3.**

Nos. 8–31 stay as the LilyPond branch delivers them unless the owner wants them replaced by the
recognised Peters versions (question 1 bears on this: LilyPond's No. 8 sits an octave below Peters'
right hand and puts the left in bass clef where Peters has both in treble).

## Traps

- **Beat counts read as fingers** — the digits under the first bar of the early pieces. Off by
  switch; a human capturing digits must skip them too.
- **The teacher's pages recognise as pieces of their own.** A Seconda page exports as movements like
  any other; the manifest's page map is what pairs it with its Prima and keeps it out of the derived
  file.
- **Stacked fingering on chords** (p. 76, "4 / 2 / 1" over a triad) belongs to the chord's notes
  top to bottom; `parseScore` reads the first `technical > fingering` of each note
  (`parseScore.ts:169`), so each note must carry its own.
- **8va lines.** MusicXML pitches under an `<octave-shift>` are the sounding pitches, which is what
  `parseScore` wants; whether Audiveris reads the line at all is a proofreading item, not an
  assumption.
- **Grace notes** are skipped by `parseScore` (`:94`); they stay in the file for the staff and are
  neither fingered by the rule nor counted by the check.
- **Pitch range.** The default keyboard preset stops at B5 (`src/config.ts:13`); the 8va passages
  and No. 109 go above it. Harmless to this step — nothing bundles these files yet — and a trap for
  step 22's check that every pitch fits every preset.

## What this makes harder later

**Committed files are the one-way part**: around two hundred source and derived files — some 85
numbers outside 8–31, Nos. 1 and 2 split into their variations — whose names use the Peters
numbers. Four futures were played forward.

**The child learns from a different edition** — a Polish or German print with its own numbering and
its own fingering. The notes are Beyer's and survive; the numbers need a mapping and the digits are
Ruthardt's, not theirs. This is question 1, asked before a hundred files are named.

**Step 22 with Beyer as the library.** A row of radio buttons holds a handful of titles, not a
hundred; the preset range and the one-part, staff-2-is-left parser rules above all bite there. None
of it is built here, and each is written down so step 22 does not discover it.

**The app learns repeats, or plays the teacher's part.** Both are regenerations from the source
files rather than re-recognition — the reason there are two files.

**A better fingering rule** — thumb-under, stretches. Regenerate the derived files; the book's
digits in the sources do not move. What would have to be undone is any hand-edited derived file,
which is why there are none.

## Open questions for the owner

Ranked by what a wrong answer costs.

1. **Which book is "the original book"?** The fingering is meant to be "as it was in the original
   book". (a) **The Peters/Ruthardt edition this step recognises** — its numbers and digits are the
   ones in the files. (b) **The copy the child actually uses** — then its numbering needs mapping and
   its digits, if they differ, are the ones that count, and only the owner can read them; if that
   copy is a modern edition, its fingering may be in copyright and must not be committed.
   _Recommend (a)_, with item 11's manual read against the child's copy noting where they differ.
   _If wrong_: the numbers and fingers on screen disagree with the book on the music stand, for a
   child.
2. **Who proofreads, and when does a piece count as done?** (a) **The owner reads every piece against
   the scan in Audiveris's editor before it is committed** — thorough, and roughly eighty pages of
   the owner's time. (b) **The agent's checks point at suspect bars, pieces are committed marked
   unproofread, and the owner confirms each one the first time the child plays it.** (c) **Only
   pieces with no suspect bar are committed**; the rest wait. _Recommend deciding after the
   calibration batch reports its error rate; lean (b)._ _If wrong_: (b) — a wrong note practised until
   someone hears it; (a) — hours spent confirming pieces that were already right.
3. **The book's printed digits, and notes the rule cannot reach.** (a) **Capture the printed digits
   as anchors during proofreading** — the owner, or the agent reading the scan and the owner
   confirming — and finger any unreachable note by hand in the source. (b) **Generated fingering
   only**; a piece with unreachable notes waits until the rule is extended. (c) **`pianoplayer`
   fills the unreachable notes.** _Recommend (a)._ _If wrong_: (a) — more reading per page;
   (b) — the unanchored rule contradicts the book's left hand from No. 8 on, and most pieces from the
   scales onward wait; (c) — fingering that moves the hand where the book does not.
4. **How much of the book?** (a) **Numbered pieces only, the pupil's part**: each variation of Nos. 1
   and 2 a file of its own, each duet its Prima, the teacher's part kept in the source. (b) (a) plus
   **the appendix finger exercises and the 24 scales**. (c) (a) without **Nos. 1 and 2**, whose
   one-hand variations against a teacher's part make least sense without the teacher. _Recommend
   (a)._ _If wrong_: pages recognised and proofread for nothing, or a gap found when a lesson reaches
   it.
5. **Where does Audiveris run?** (a) **The owner's host**, recognition and corrections both — an
   install on their machine. (b) **Batch recognition in the container**, no install for the owner,
   corrections made in the MusicXML rather than in Audiveris's editor. (c) (b), with the editor on the
   host only if question 2 lands on (a). _Recommend (c)_, falling back to (a) if the Ubuntu package
   will not run in the Debian container. _If wrong_: an install nobody needed, or an
   editor-less correction path that is slower than the one it replaced.

Questions 2 and 3 **rewrite what batches 2 onward do**, so those batches do not start until they are
answered. The calibration batch holds under every answer, provided question 5 has one.

## Decisions to record in `DECISIONS.md`

- The source edition and why the others were rejected — resolution, EU copyright status of the
  Italian editors, provenance; that files use the Peters numbers, which run to 109.
- Two files per piece, source and derived, and that human corrections go into the source.
- Fingering: printed digits are anchors, the five-finger rule fills between them, unreachable notes
  are listed rather than guessed; `pianoplayer` and the unanchored rule rejected with the numbers
  above.
- Repeats written out in derived files, extending the Cicha Noc entry from one piece to a library.
- A left-hand-alone piece's notes sit on staff 2; a derived file carries only the pupil's part.
- Audiveris's fingering switch stays off, and why.

## Gate

- **Layer 1, over the library** (Vitest, so it runs in `npm run ci`): every derived file the manifest
  lists exists, parses with the app's own `parseScore`, has at least one event, and — for each hand
  the manifest says plays — has a finger on every pitched non-grace note; notes of a hand the
  manifest says does not play are absent. Every file's `<rights>` names the edition, the IMSLP
  number and the pages. **The listing is what fails if a batch's pieces are missing**, and the
  finger assertion is what fails if the fingering pass never ran.
- **Layer 1, over the fingering**: every digit in a source file is the finger of the same note in
  the derived file — **the check that fails if the rule overwrote the book** — and each piece's first
  position agrees with the hand-position diagram the manifest records from the scan. Nos. 8 and 9's
  left hands (2 and 3 on G) are the discriminating cases: the unanchored rule fails both.
- **Measured, not asserted**: the calibration batch's note-level agreement with the LilyPond
  transcriptions of Nos. 8–31, reported to the owner. It is a measurement of the OMR, not a pass mark.
- `npm run ci` green.
- **What no test can prove** is that a recognised note is the printed one. That is the proofreading,
  and it is manual.
